# panelization-bench

A benchmark that asks: **which 3D application is genuinely best to drive with an AI
agent?** — measured on one objective, millimetre-scored task (planar-quad
panelization of a doubly-curved canopy), and designed to **expose where MCP servers
fail** rather than to be passed. The transcript of every run is the product: a
catalog of failures that is the spec for the MCP servers to build next.

## What this benchmark actually measures (the thesis)

It is **not**, primarily, a test of the model's geometric reasoning. The
`ezdxf-control` lane (a headless DXF library, no application) isolates that, and any
capable model reaches the same geometric wall in it.

The benchmark compares the geometry and tooling an agent can reach through each
engine. Global PQ optimizers (for example Kangaroo/ShapeOp) are candidate tools,
but the shipped experiments do not establish that a particular optimizer or
remeshing method is necessary. A PASS reports the scorer's checks, not the
algorithm used or a proof of fabrication readiness.

## The task

Panelize a 40 m × 20 m saddle canopy into quad panels at a ~1.5 m module, every panel
planar within **5 mm**, export the geometry, and report panel count, max planarity
deviation, and how many panels fail tolerance. The full brief given to the agent under
test is `prompt/task_prompt.v1.md` (engine-neutral, versioned).

The task is chosen because it is scored objectively in millimetres (no taste), it is a
real constraint that decides whether a facade gets built, and — critically — **a weak
agent will panelize, declare success, and never measure planarity.** The benchmark is
built to catch exactly that.

## The surface

```
z(x, y) = 2.5·(x/20)·(y/10)  +  0.6·sin(3π·x/20)·cos(2π·y/10)      (meters)
```

A hypar base (genuine double curvature, the classic saddle canopy) plus an anisotropic
corrugation. Definition lives in `reference/surface_def.py`; the importable mesh and
provenance are generated by `reference/generate_surface.py`.

**Why not a pure hypar?** A pure hypar has globally *constant* twist, so its optimal
planar-quad direction is globally obvious and uniform — a smart panelizer and a dumb
one converge on the same answer, and the raw defect is a tidy uniform ~14 mm. The
corrugation makes the twist **change sign across the field** (measured range ≈ [−0.15,
+0.19]), so the local conjugate/asymptotic directions **rotate** and no single global
alignment planarizes the surface. Measured on the naive UV grid at 1.5 m: max **103 mm**
planarity, **327 / 351 panels (93 %) fail** 5 mm, non-uniformly — while quality,
coverage and module are all perfect. That is the "panelize and declare victory" trap,
caught by the scorer as `PLANARITY_FAIL`.

**Curvature.** Negative Gaussian curvature over ≈ 50 % of the surface (mixed
anticlastic/synclastic — the corrugation's crests/troughs are locally synclastic; this
is structural for any sinusoidal corrugation and is realistic for freeform canopies).
The surface has parabolic K = 0 loci and also flat points where both principal
curvatures vanish. For example, at x = 0, y = 0.11207710319723743 m, all three
Hessian entries are zero. These are umbilic degeneracies: principal directions
are not uniquely defined there. Pointwise K = 0 does not imply a developable
neighborhood or a cylindrical patch, and no claim of benign meshing behavior
follows from it. Earlier monkey-saddle comparisons have no shipped reproduction
and do not establish that this surface avoids such degeneracies.

## Reference examples and their limits

The shipped examples illustrate planarity, quality and coverage tradeoffs. They do
not establish a feasibility bound for the whole canopy at a 1.5 m module.
`reference/build_reference_solution.py` rebuilds the two reference nets; the
harness selftest rebuilds the naive grid. Measurements below are on saved OBJ
coordinates, using the scorer's SVD metric:

| example | max planarity | quality | coverage and module |
|---|---|---|---|
| naive 27×13 UV grid | 103 mm | median min angle 88°, 0 slivers | whole canopy, ~1.5 m |
| principal curvature-line patch | 0.000445 mm | median min angle 87°, 0 slivers | 1.78×1.92 m patch, **0.078 m median edge** |
| arbitrary conjugate net | 0.000505 mm | median min angle 1.3°, 310/351 slivers | area ratio 0.086; fails coverage |

The small principal patch demonstrates planar quads with good angles at an 8 cm
module, far below the permitted module band. It is not evidence that the same
quality is achievable at 1.5 m across the full canopy. The collapsed conjugate
net demonstrates why planarity alone is insufficient.

**Reference artifacts** in `reference/` are examples, never scoring targets:
`canopy_gold_principal_patch.obj` is the small patch and `canopy_gold_planar.obj`
is the collapsed net. Scoring never rewards similarity to them.

Earlier exploratory notes reported guided projection, four fixed-grid planarizers,
and seeding/corrugation sweeps. Their scripts, parameters and results are not
included here, so those observations are not reproducible evidence for solver
limits or near-bimodal behavior. No such general conclusion follows from the
shipped examples. **Tolerance stays at 5 mm** as the task specification, not as
an experimentally established discontinuity.

## Scoring — `score.py` (the neutral judge)

Geometry is judged **outside every engine**, on the exported artifact, by one numpy
script. Rhino is not scored with Rhino's tools and Blender with Blender's — that would
compare measurement code, not engines. An agent can only pass by actually solving the
problem, so four independent constraints are enforced, each recorded with its numbers:

- **PLANARITY** — per quad, max distance of its 4 vertices to their best-fit plane
  (SVD), in mm. Tolerance 5 mm.
- **MODULE** — median panel edge length must sit in the 0.9–2.4 m band (kills the
  subdivide-to-0.2 m dodge, where planarity is free but fabrication cost explodes).
- **COVERAGE** — panel-area ratio to the reference must be 0.85–1.15, with largest
  surface-sample-to-panel gap at most 2 m. The symmetric 15% area allowance rejects
  both substantial missing area and duplicate full layers; it is not an overlap proof.
- **OFF-SURFACE** — exact analytic vertical deviation `|z_v − f(x_v,y_v)|` (no
  triangulation noise floor). A vertex-pair heuristic flags pairs within 1 mm in
  plan and over 50 mm apart in height. It does not detect general projected-face
  overlaps or certify that a mesh is a height field; shifted layers and folded
  strips can evade this heuristic.

With a reference, the mesh must also form one connected manifold canopy with
matching Euler characteristic. Per-panel vertex copies are welded on a 1 µm
coordinate grid; open interior seams, holes and disconnected panels fail
`INVALID_CONNECTIVITY`. Boundary vertices and edge midpoints must match the
reference boundary in plan in both directions within 0.25 m (the off-surface
allowance). Topology par is computed from the reference's corners **and** Euler
characteristic, never from the submission's component count.

Every face must be a quad: any triangles trigger `DODGE_TRIANGULATED`; other
non-quad panels trigger `NON_QUAD_PANELS`. Mixed-face meshes cannot pass.
**Quality** (min interior angle, aspect ratio, degenerate-panel count) is measured and
recorded but is a **graded axis, not a hard gate** — full-task feasibility at buildable quality is not established; egregious collapse still trips
`DEGENERATE_PANELS`. Every run is classified by which trilemma corner it landed on
(`trilemma_corner`), so a sophisticated 2×2 m principal patch is distinguishable from
an agent that panelized a random tenth of the surface and quit.

The scorer CLI requires `--units` (use `auto` to guess with a loud warning) — a scorer that
silently compares mm to m is worthless. `score.py` is covered by 16 exact-answer tests
(a saddle quad reads its analytic deviation `h` exactly; a tilted planar quad reads 0,
proving best-fit-plane SVD; the unit trap; point-to-triangle by hand; every cheat
verdict).

## Harness — `runner.py`

Each run produces a record with four **un-merged** blocks so "better geometry" is never
confused with "reachable by an agent":

- **stack** — exact version of every moving part (MCP server, host app, plugin/backend,
  driving model). Version skew silently corrupts a comparison, so an undeterminable part
  is a **visible `unknown` plus a warning**, never omitted.
- **reachability** — the setup-friction axis: `human_steps_to_ready`,
  `requires_host_app_running`, `time_to_first_successful_tool_call`. An engine an agent
  cannot reach without a human is worse at being driven, even with better geometry.
- **catalog** — the tool-call transcript distilled (per-tool counts, errors, timing,
  dead-ends). This is the failure catalog.
- **geometry** — `score.py`'s verdict.

Run-level verdicts are kept distinct: `NEVER_REACHED_ENGINE` (transport/setup),
`REACHED_TASK_FAILED` (reached, gave up), **`FALSE_SUCCESS`** (declared success,
geometry failed — the motivating case, detected by cross-checking the agent's claim
against the independent score), `PASS_NO_SELF_CHECK` (good geometry, never measured —
may be luck), and `PASS` (good geometry AND measured). A geometric
`PASS_UNBUILDABLE` remains `PASS_UNBUILDABLE` at run level, or
`PASS_UNBUILDABLE_NO_SELF_CHECK` when the agent did not report planarity,
regardless of its success claim.

**Lanes** (`runner.py list-lanes`): `ezdxf-control` is the **control — a DXF library,
not an application** (0 human steps, no app, the zero point on the friction axis);
`blender` (addon autostarts, app must be open); `rhino-community` / `rhino-mcneel` (one
`mcpstart` per session, possible first-load approval). Friction ranking, per the PC
survey in `pc/PC-ENGINES.md`: ezdxf (no app) > 3ds Max (autostart) > Rhino (one
command) > Cinema 4D (GUI click).

Because the control lane needs no app, the whole harness is exercisable now:

```
python3 runner.py selftest      # drives ezdxf end-to-end -> FALSE_SUCCESS (naive grid)
```

Tool readiness uses the first successful call's completion time. Create the
`ToolCallLogger` before setup and call `logger.finish()` after the final export;
its adjacent `.jsonl.timing.json` file persists actual start/end timestamps for
`evaluate`. Legacy transcripts without complete timing metadata use available
call bounds and emit a warning: unrecorded setup/finalization time is unknown.

## Layout

```
reference/
  surface_def.py               analytic surface (single source of truth)
  generate_surface.py          -> canopy_reference.obj + surface_params.json
  build_reference_solution.py  -> the two gold artifacts (existence proofs, not targets)
score.py                       neutral geometry judge
runner.py                      run harness (stack / reachability / catalog / verdicts)
prompt/task_prompt.v1.md       the engine-neutral task brief (versioned)
tests/                         25 tests (16 scorer exact-answer, 9 harness)
pc/PC-ENGINES.md               sibling: PC engine eligibility + friction matrix
requirements.txt               pinned deps (the benchmark must not drift under a bump)
```

## Reproduce

```
pip install -r requirements.txt
python3 reference/generate_surface.py
python3 reference/build_reference_solution.py
python3 -m pytest tests/ -q
python3 runner.py selftest
# score a supported exported mesh:
python3 score.py <mesh.obj|.ply|.dxf|.3dm> \
    --reference reference/canopy_reference.obj \
    --surface-def reference/surface_def.py --units m
```

Supported input is polygonal OBJ, **ASCII PLY** (binary PLY is rejected), DXF
3DFACE/MESH/POLYFACE entities, or **mesh geometry in 3DM** (Brep/NURBS-only
files are rejected; mesh them before export). Select ASCII when exporting PLY.

Everything is deterministic and version-pinned so the benchmark cannot silently change
its own results — the same principle as the per-run `stack` block, one level down.
Do **not** run against live engines without opening the app first; that is the
orchestrator's call.

## Run log

### 2026-08-15 — first live run: Blender 4.5.12 + frontier model

**Verdict: `PASS_UNBUILDABLE`** · trilemma corner `PLANAR+CONFORMING (slivers)`

| metric | value |
|---|---|
| planarity | max **3.49 mm**, 0/351 over the 5 mm tolerance |
| off-surface | **0.00 mm** (analytic — vertices exactly on the surface) |
| coverage | area ratio 1.01, max gap 0.59 m |
| module | median edge 1.79 m (in band) |
| quality | worst interior angle **0.0°**, worst aspect **3.6e12**, **4 degenerate panels** |

The agent rebuilt the surface from the closed form, confirmed a naive 1.5 m grid fails
at 103 mm, then warped the grid toward a conjugate net using interior-point
Levenberg–Marquardt with a log-barrier on panel area. It reported the result honestly,
including the cost.

**Two findings from this run:**

1. **`PLANAR+CONFORMING` is reachable on this surface after all.** The reference build
   had concluded otherwise, because the arbitrary Cauchy-seeded conjugate net *collapses*
   here (area ratio 0.09). A barrier-constrained optimizer gets there while covering the
   footprint — at the cost of quality. This is one reported outcome, not proof of a general trilemma.

2. **The scorer had a hole, now fixed.** Quality was measured but never reached the
   verdict, so this mesh initially scored a clean `PASS` — a facade with a 3.6-trillion-to-one
   aspect ratio read as a win. Quality is still deliberately *not* a hard gate (no
   planar+quality+conforming solution is known at this module, so failing an engine for it
   would be unfair), but a `PASS` earned by slivering now qualifies to `PASS_UNBUILDABLE`.
   Regression test: `test_sliver_gaming_is_not_a_clean_pass`.

**⚠️ This run is contaminated and must be re-run before it counts as a clean data point.**
The agent read `reference/canopy_gold_*.obj` during the attempt — by its own account "to
calibrate feasibility, not to derive geometry," but it was not blind. Future runs must
deny access to `reference/canopy_gold_*` and to this run log.
